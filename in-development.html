Status

In development

Active work · citizen-led · not a government portal

Civics.au and the connected WebCivics work sit in active development. What you see here is living material: proposals, writing, and infrastructure notes that are still being shaped. This is not a finished product release, and it is not a government or civic-agency service.

Why AI is in the loop

To improve both capacity and outcome, AI is used to support development. That support runs under a nominal budget. The goal is to move the work forward with limited human time, not to outsource authorship or accountability.

Humans remain responsible for the project and for final outcomes — including intent, voice, and what is published as civics (citizenship and citizen-led public-good work). That responsibility does not mean AI always behaves as asked. AI can draft content that later turns out to show the opposite of the intended intents and purposes. Finding that inversion can take time; addressing it can take more time still, especially when review and repair depend on available budget.

In that sense AI can interfere in undesirable ways, much as adversaries or malware can cause problems: not because the project intends harm, but because automated assistance can inject, invert, or obscure material until a human catches and corrects it.

What can go wrong with AI-assisted development

AI assistance can help and it can also fail in ways that look finished when they are not. Known issues include:

  • Inverted intent. Generated or revised text can say the opposite of the author’s intended intents and purposes, or soften a hard language lock (for example, sliding from civics into civic or government framing). That failure may not be obvious at once; discovery and correction can lag.
  • False completion. Systems or agents can claim a job is done when it is not: missing pages, broken links, untested paths, or “PASS” labels without a real check.
  • Thin work presented as done. Draft, stub, or placeholder material can be framed as complete when it still needs review, amendment, or replacement.
  • Invented detail. Numbers, sources, menus, or technical claims can appear that were never verified against a real file, tip, or source.
  • Drift. Constraints that were clear in one pass (voice, licence, capability profile) can be dropped or rewritten in a later pass without notice.
  • Extractive framing. AI can frame work so that humans are expected to self-fund the costs of life and work resources, while useful derivatives of that work are provided freely to foreign and domestic users — shifting cost onto people and value outward, often without stating that bargain plainly.
  • Dependence and unencumbered tokens. Copy can push dependence on AI agents and/or platforms as the only useful work provider, while asking that input tokens (prompts, data, knowledge, labour) be given on an unencumbered “copyleft”-style basis — easy to take, hard to reclaim stewardship over.
  • Political narrative without STEM diligence. Political or policy claims can appear that do not survive diligent, methods-based STEM investigation: no clear hypothesis, evidence, measurement, or falsifiable check — only confident prose.
  • Consent and provenance wash. Sources, licences, and human authorship can be blurred or omitted so material looks free to reuse when it is not, or so a human’s prior work is absorbed without clear credit or terms.
  • Safety theatre. Warnings, disclaimers, or “ethics” language can be generated that sound careful while leaving the harmful pattern intact — compliance-looking text that does not change behaviour.
  • Metric gaming. Progress can be reported in tokens, commits, or “done” counts that look green while capacity, correctness, and citizen-useful outcome stay flat or worse.

These are reasons to treat the site as under development and to prefer human review before treating any claim as settled.

Budget and repair

The AI support budget can be exhausted. When that happens, errors may not be identified, amended, or resolved until budget is available again. Gaps can sit in public view longer than anyone would like. That is a practical limit of nominal spend, not a promise that every issue will be fixed on a fixed calendar.

Toward a more formal release

Overall status remains active development. Moving toward a more formal release-level status of completion will take some time. How fast that advance happens depends on budget available for review, correction, and further build work. Until then, please read this site as work in progress: useful where it is solid, provisional where it is not, and open to correction.
